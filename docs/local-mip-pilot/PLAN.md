# Investigating LocalMIP and FPR in LP-IP-craft

**Decision plan · 26 November 2026 · Priority: investigate now · Engineering budget: 2–4 days**

Does a short, LP-free repair search at the first fractional root give LP-IP-craft a checked incumbent sooner, or a better incumbent within the same solve budget? Implement one LocalMIP neighbourhood behind an opt-in Rust setting. Compare it with the existing root stack, including Feasibility Jump (FJ), under both a common solve limit and a matched local-search allowance. Use the results to decide whether to retain the prototype and whether Fix, Propagate, and Repair (FPR) deserves a separate experiment.

The first deliverable is a decision backed by per-instance traces. A clean negative result is sufficient to close the pilot. The 233-instance replicated campaign belongs on the home machines **after January 2027**.

## 1. What the evidence supports

Simon Spoorendonk’s September preprint is indexed as *Presolve heuristics in HiGHS: implementation and computational study*. FPR means **Fix, Propagate, and Repair**. It fixes a variable, propagates the consequences, repairs or backtracks when a choice fails, and can repair the completed assignment. LP-IP-craft’s L1 feasibility pump searches for feasibility by alternating rounding and LP projection. The MIT-licensed companion repository implements FJ, FPR, LocalMIP and Scylla. The configuration behind the headline had FJ, presolve FPR and LocalMIP enabled. Scylla and the LP-guided FPR variant had zero effort. [Preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22938) · [Headline protocol and configuration](https://github.com/spoorendonk/mip-heuristics/blob/4e3bf0021c178189b26cce19ca65cbe78470d5e3/bench/headline/README.md) · [License](https://github.com/spoorendonk/mip-heuristics/blob/4e3bf0021c178189b26cce19ca65cbe78470d5e3/LICENSE)

## 3. Prototype specification

### A shared budget and patience runner

Add one opt-in effort setting to `NativeOptions` (proposed name `local_mip_effort`, default zero). Store pilot controls for patience, seed and control-arm selection in the native benchmark driver. With zero effort, bypass setup and allocation. Use one worker and one attempt at the top-level serial root. Explicitly suppress dispatch in RENS children, component subsolves and tree or packing subproblems. Emit a skip reason when the solver reaches an integral root, never reaches a usable root LP, or takes an ineligible path. Retain these cases in the end-to-end comparison.

Reuse the solver’s existing deadline, cancellation token and pivot counter. Mirror upstream’s separation between **hard effort** and **effort since progress**. Start with an allowance of `min(2_000_000, 64 × max(nnz, 1))` coefficient visits and patience of one quarter of that allowance. These are proposed CI settings. Upstream expresses its budgets in multiples of `nnz × 1024`. Compute with saturating arithmetic. Zero patience disables only the stale check. The proposed hard cap for a local stage is 250 ms or the remaining solve time, whichever is smaller. All limits include setup.

Charge incidence construction, activity refreshes, candidate generation, neighbour scoring and applied updates. Count per-row/column bookkeeping and checker cost separately so sparse scans or repeated validation cannot disappear from the timing. Check remaining quota before a scan or in bounded chunks, discard incompletely scored moves, and poll deadlines within long rows. A local stop returns control to the solver. A global time, cancellation, node or iteration stop follows the existing solve contract. The neighbourhood requests **zero LP pivots and zero search nodes**. Attribute subsequent certification LPs, root solves and RENS work to their actual callers. Avoid a C++ link dependency. Retain the upstream MIT notice for any translated source.

Reset the patience counter on a new best violated-row count that is positive progress and at most 90% of the count at the previous reset. Register progress on the last move before testing patience. Apply the hard limit even if that move earned a reset. Preserve any checked incumbent obtained by the deadline. Record completion, stalled, effort exhausted, local timeout, global stop and numerical rejection separately.

### One neighbourhood: a tight move on a violated row

1. Start from the accepted fractional root LP vector. Round integer columns into the integral hull of each bound interval. Retain bounded continuous values. Compute row activities, a list of violated rows and column incidence lists once. Reject inconsistent or non-finite state cleanly.
2. Use a reproducibly seeded sample of up to eight violated rows and at most 64 row–column proposals per step. For each nonzero coefficient, move **one** column toward the violated finite row side. For row activity `lhs[i]`, violated bound `b` and coefficient `a[i,j]`, the boundary value is `x[j] + (b - lhs[i]) / a[i,j]`. For integers, consider the floor and ceiling inside the integral box. For continuous columns, use the clipped boundary value. Remove duplicate and zero-length moves.
3. Score only incident rows. Primarily reward weighted rows repaired or penalise newly broken rows. For rows that stay violated, count a meaningful reduction or increase once, independent of its magnitude. Break ties by the sense-correct objective change and seeded order. Give the reverse direction a short tabu tenure (initially three accepted moves). On a plateau, increment weights on sampled violated rows and allow the best admissible non-improving move. Start all row weights at one and cap them at 1,024. Log the tenure and cap. This differs materially from native FJ’s weighted sum of violation **magnitudes**.
4. Update affected activities, record effort, and periodically recompute activities from CSR to bound drift. On apparent feasibility, recheck on the original model, recompute the objective including its offset, and submit the proposal if eligible. End this attempt on its first checked feasible point, including a point worse than a pre-existing incumbent. Count the latter as feasible but non-improving. A rejected apparent solution triggers one full refresh before a numerical exit if the discrepancy persists.
5. With an existing checked incumbent, still start from the rounded LP. This tests whether an independently repaired vector can beat it. The prototype has no feasible-point lifting or multistart phase. An improvement deficit on already-feasible cases therefore leaves a specific follow-up question.

The directly borrowed mechanism is the sampled tight-move phase. The scoring simplification, LP root seed, single attempt, pilot caps and post-feasibility stop are deliberate Rust adaptations. Record them in the implementation log. A restricted sub-MIP around the incumbent would overlap RENS and would answer a different question. [LocalMIP paper](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.CP.2024.19) · [Reference move generation and scoring](https://github.com/spoorendonk/mip-heuristics/blob/4e3bf0021c178189b26cce19ca65cbe78470d5e3/src/local_mip_search.cpp) · [Native FJ](https://github.com/dietmarwo/LP-IP-craft/blob/2468f091855a00085ad39e3c6f7922732f22fda4/crates/native/src/jump.rs)

## 6. What to measure

**Single clock and independent admission.** Start the monotonic clock at native solve entry after file I/O, before presolve. At every first or improving original-model-checked incumbent, retain time, objective, source, phase, cumulative pivots and nodes. Include discoveries from diving, pump, RENS, FJ, LocalMIP and the tree. At a RENS boundary, credit the first original-model validation when the child returns the point. Avoid recording the same vector again as a warm start. Track certified-bound milestones separately. Preserve time spent checking. Keep existing diagnostic schemas compatible and stream or integrate improvements online so a full trace buffer cannot silently improve or worsen the measured integral.

| Metric | Definition and failure treatment |
|---|---|
| First checked incumbent | Elapsed time to the first accepted original-model vector. Report found/eligible counts at the root, 1 s, 5 s and 30 s, paired gain/loss counts, and capped time `min(t_first, T)`. Runs with no incumbent are right-censored. Report objective quality on jointly feasible pairs with its denominator. |
| Primal integral (primary) | Follow the penalty definition below. Integrate the checked incumbent trajectory from solve entry to the common horizon, including time before any feasible point. A complete eligible run with no incumbent scores 2. |
| Incumbent quality and proof progress | Record the original objective at root exit, 1 s, 5 s and 30 s. Report first and final sources, checked and certified coverage, native safe bound/gap when available, and terminal status. A benchmark reference gap and a certified solver gap answer different questions. |
| Work and opportunity cost | Record pivots/nodes at first incumbent, root exit and solve end. Break out root LP, pump/dives, RENS, FJ, LocalMIP, certification and tree effort where applicable. Include setup, row/coefficient visits, proposed/applied moves, stale resets, stopping reason, elapsed time and peak RSS. Report fraction reaching the hook and distinct instances improved. |

For the primal integral, take the frozen reference objective and include the original objective offset. Orient both values to minimisation for native maximisation cases. Before any incumbent, the penalty is 2. For opposite signs it is 1. Otherwise compute `max(0, round((z - ref) / max(1, abs(z), abs(ref)), 6))`. The normalised integral is the area under this step function on `[0, T)` divided by `T`. Extend the last recorded penalty to the horizon when a solve ends early. Flag any checked point that beats the reference for review.

Score with the benchmark’s current six-decimal penalty implementation. Report public-reference scores separately because their references come from LP-IP-craft. For each instance, average the paired log differences across its seeds at shift 0.001, then exponentiate the unweighted mean across instances to get a ratio (lower is better). Also publish per-arm shifted geometric means, per-case ratios, better/tied/worse counts and leave-one-instance-out ratios. Bootstrap **instances** with all their seed pairs kept together for a descriptive 95% interval. Publish raw pair outcomes and show censored counts. A small validation sample cannot support a default-promotion claim. [Reference scorer](https://github.com/spoorendonk/mip-heuristics/blob/4e3bf0021c178189b26cce19ca65cbe78470d5e3/bench/parse_highs_log.py) · [GAMS benchmark protocol](https://www.gams.com/blog/2026/03/expanding-the-focus-introducing-the-mipfeas-benchmark/)

Keep unsupported inputs outside the eligible performance denominator and publish them in the coverage table. Keep eligible runs that never reach the hook in the primary comparison. For a crash, dropped trace or forced kill, publish the failure, retain any validated partial prefix, flag the paired comparison as incomplete and show a worst-case sensitivity assigning penalty 2 for the unobserved tail. An implementation-induced failure blocks advancement.

## 7. Tests, decisions and handoff

**Checks before timing**

- Replay stock vs new/effort-zero on a deterministic node/pivot-limited corpus. Require the same status, checked objective, safe bound, nodes, pivots and ordering of incumbent sources after normalising wall times. Test that disabled dispatch performs no setup and that nested solves cannot launch the worker.
- Use tiny enumerable mixed/binary cases to check lower/upper/ranged/equality rows, negative coefficients, fractional integer bounds, fixed columns, both objective senses, offsets, and tolerance-boundary violations. Verify incremental and recomputed activities after accepted moves. Independently evaluate every fixture proposal.
- Exercise zero effort, zero patience, a last-step 10% reduction, a plateau, cancellation during a dense scan, node/pivot limits already spent, and a hard cap coinciding with a patience reset. The previous checked incumbent survives every local stop.
- Unit-test trace integration. At a horizon of 10 s, with reference 4, first objective 8 at 2 s and objective 4 at 6 s, the normalised integral is **0.6**. No incumbent scores **2**. Add early completion, negative/opposite signs, zero objective, maximisation and a RENS-to-warm-start duplicate. Verify the online total against a replay of all improvements.
- Run the repository’s locked Rust tests, formatter and clippy checks. Run original-model checker and oracle regression gates for the affected MIP paths. Pick one repeatable improvement and one regression or no-op for trace-level inspection.

**Decision rules, fixed before opening the Rust holdout.** These are proposed engineering thresholds for spending more time, not expected effects.

| Outcome | Action |
|---|---|
| Any invalid admission, altered proof status without justification, lost incumbent, broken limit, nondeterministic work under fixed work/seed, or unexplained effort-zero drift | Fix and rerun before interpreting speed. |
| Held-out B/A integral ratio ≤0.95, checked coverage at 30 s at least equal, and the direction persists after removing the largest beneficiary; or repeatable new checked coverage on at least two held-out instances without a net loss of final coverage | Retain opt-in for the larger campaign. Require an attributable LocalMIP find and inspect D/C and the worst regressions. If matched FJ achieves the same gain, prioritise FJ budgeting first. |
| Mixed or marginal signal, a single dominating family, or too few eligible roots to exercise the move | Publish the unresolved mechanism and its counts. Keep the patch experimental. If roots seldom complete, direct the next measurement to root-LP time or input support. |
| Adequate dispatch, no additional coverage or quality, matched FJ as good or better and no repeated family-specific gain | Close or archive the neighbourhood. Keep the scorer if it demonstrably improves measurement of other heuristics. |

**FPR decision.** During inspection, map upstream `fpr_core.cpp`, `prop_engine.cpp`, `repair_search.cpp` and `walksat.cpp` to native `propagate.rs` and `mixed.rs`. Record supported row types, what can be rolled back, how a partial assignment detects contradiction, and which propagation events can count toward the common effort budget. A useful next test would be Class-1 FPR on cases where several integer decisions must be coordinated and single-column repair repeatedly stalls. Advance it to its own plan if at least two such **eligible** instances still miss a checked incumbent and a short trace shows a fixing, propagation and backtrack can escape the stall. If feasibility already exists but certification dominates, pursue the witness path instead. Scope the next FPR test to one fixing policy, reversible local domains and bounded repair, and compare it against the same baselines.
